Summary.

Motilal Oswal, a legacy fintech company, set out to close the financial literacy gap in India's tier-2 and tier-3 cities. We designed a learning platform that made complex investment concepts accessible and comprehensible, by simplifying financial jargon and delivering content in multiple vernacular languages.

This initiative not only broadened access to financial education but also empowered a wider audience to make confident investment decisions.

The goal was to build a learning ecosystem that keeps users engaged, nurtures financial literacy, and drives conversions to demat account sign-ups on Motilal Oswal's main investment platform.

My role.

I was responsible for driving the UX strategy: leading research, facilitating stakeholder discussions, and turning insights into actionable design directions. Managing a team of three designers, I ensured our solutions balanced user needs with business growth goals, while collaborating closely with product managers on execution.

Problem statement.

Novice investors often lack the confidence to make informed investment decisions, which limits their willingness to take calculated risks and learn through experience. Motilal Oswal wanted to address:

Low retention Users weren't coming back to the existing learning content.
Limited engagement Engagement dropped away beyond the initial sign-up.
No interactive learning There was no gamified, interactive way to learn about investing.

Objectives.

Increase retention Encourage users to return through progressive learning and challenges.
Boost engagement Add interactive and gamified elements like quizzes, prediction games, and leaderboards.
Drive conversions Create smooth bridges to demat account sign-ups.

Key challenges we focused on.

Language barrier Most financial content is available only in English, making it inaccessible for users who primarily consume content in regional languages.
Low digital literacy Many users from smaller cities are less familiar with advanced digital platforms, requiring simpler interfaces and guided flows.
Engagement & retention Traditional e-learning formats often failed to hold user attention, leading to drop-offs before completing learning modules.

Landscape analysis.

Since most competitors in the market have a learning platform, it was important to understand the key features and avoid obvious mistakes. Solution highlights.

What I'm taking with me.

01
Language is access, not a feature. Supporting regional languages meant localising examples, references and formats, not just translating copy. That is what made the content genuinely usable for the people it was built for.
02
Simplicity builds confidence. Designing for first-time and less digitally confident users pushed us towards short micro-modules, a guided step-by-step path and clear progress cues, so the next step was always obvious.
03
Learning and business goals can reinforce each other. Placing conversion nudges at natural moments, like after finishing a module or a quiz, let us drive demat sign-ups without interrupting the learning experience.
