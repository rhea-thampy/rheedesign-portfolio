The brief.

This project was for one of India's top brokerage companies, with an active user base of around 10 million and 75 million transactions. The company is among the top three brokers in the country, so our design decisions could influence how Indians invest and trade.

It was an ongoing, long-term engagement: regularly updating the mobile and web platforms based on user input and competitive analysis. Over a year I worked across nine modules, including Stock SIP, KYC, profit & loss, watchlist, basket orders, onboarding, the order pad, calculators, and trades & charges. This case study focuses on the two where I made the most impact: basket orders with a floating order pad, and Stock SIP.

Working with the team.

I led the UX team, working alongside UI designers, a product consultant and several product managers. The constant back-and-forth with experts helped me understand the product from different perspectives and its complete life cycle. I was also part of user testing sessions, seeing first-hand how people interacted with our designs.

Module 1

Basket orders.

A basket order lets a user place multiple orders at the same time, across different scrips and products like equity and securities, all in one go.

Cumulative margin See the total margin required for multi-leg orders.
Saved baskets Save multi-leg orders and contracts as a basket, instead of repeating the same process.
Quick entry & exit Partially enter or exit multi-leg positions quickly.
One view to monitor Monitor multi-leg positions together as one basket.

Persona & use cases.

From user feedback and research with the product team, we defined the persona we were solving for. It kept us focused on real needs, and stopped us from going overboard with features.

Toshi, 35 India · MNC employee · Trades on desktop
Toshi has traded since his 20s. He works from home and trades on a separate system; during market hours he focuses on day trading and catches up on work after the close. He researches carefully, follows the news, and hopes to trade full-time on his own terms.
Goals
Trading as a primary or secondary income Leverage market opportunities for profit Focused work hours for work-life balance Retire early and work at will
Needs
Real-time market data & quotes Advanced charts, tools, indicators & patterns A quick, seamless order execution tool A simple, flexible, widget-style platform
Pain points
Fixed order pad: no flexibility to move it on screen Missing data: market depth, average price, OHLC, 52W high/low Bulky: the order pad takes up too much screen space No edits: contracts can't be edited during execution
Use cases
Bundling orders Add multiple items to a basket, check margin and payoff, then move and edit contracts as needed.
Executing orders Execute in one click, or leg-wise (buy first, then sell), by expiry or by value.
Adjusting positions Exit and re-enter multiple positions with one click.
Monitoring positions Monitor multiple legs as a single item, with nested positions.
Saving & editing baskets Name, schedule and edit baskets, set conditional baskets, and invoke them from any screen.

User journey.

From the requirement document, I built a journey map covering every use case and edge case, from adding to a basket and executing it, through partial execution, to how baskets show up in positions. It took a few iterations with the product team to get right.

A floating order pad.

Wireframing was iterative: each round was prototyped end to end, discussed with the product team and tested with users. Alongside basket orders, we redesigned the entire order pad. It's no longer docked to one side of the screen: the new floating order pad gives traders the flexibility to work the way they want, a step towards a console-like trading experience.

A basket can be started from the watchlist, and holds up to 20 orders to execute together.

Baskets in positions.

Baskets had to fit into the existing positions page without changing its structure. Because a basket holds several orders, the table could only show so much, so each basket collapses into one row that expands into its legs.

Module 2

Stock SIP.

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, in a stock or a mutual fund.

The client had already designed a Stock SIP flow but wasn't happy with it, and needed it reworked to be more user-friendly. I started by using SIPs on different platforms myself, which doubled as a competitive analysis.

Narrowing the scope, then wireframing.

What we learnt
The existing design wasn't intuitive. First-time users who don't know what an SIP is may never use it without proper onboarding. Users needed more options while creating SIPs.
How the wireframes answered it
Less cognitive load for a quicker journey Important information upfront Clear visual hierarchy by grouping elements

I shared wireframes with the product team for feedback on layout and flow, and mapped the full SIP creation journey: every entry point, and the different paths for different user types.

The final SIP flow.

After a few rounds of iteration with the product team: create an SIP by quantity or amount, pick a frequency and date, review, and confirm, then manage, modify or pause SIPs and their e-mandates from one place.

Testing & results.

I worked with the development team to test every implementation for missing interactions or broken flows, verified that design elements were built correctly, and fed issues and improvement ideas back to the team.

Since launch, we've seen a significant decrease in customer complaints. Involving customers throughout the design process built trust in the platform, and they now give constructive feedback on every new feature.

What I'm taking with me.

01
Launch big features as an MVP. A minimum viable product makes it easier for users to adapt to new changes in a system they're already used to.
02
Test with users at every step. User testing is the clearest way to know whether a design decision actually works for the people using it.
03
Multidisciplinary teams make well-rounded solutions. Working with UI, product and consulting experts meant our solutions covered every kind of user need.
