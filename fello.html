Summary.

As Fello evolved from a marketing CRM extension into an AI-powered real estate platform, I worked on the communication infrastructure that let teams call, text and email their clients directly through Fello.

My core work was the phone number and calling setup: purchasing numbers and Copilot Seats for a team, number porting, and the compliance flows behind them. Alongside it I designed team management (seats, permissions, number assignment) and call settings at both individual and admin levels.

Each of these involved long, regulation-bound flows that had to feel simple for a non-technical admin to complete. The deep-dive below walks through the piece I owned most directly.

The problem I owned.

Before a team could make a single call, text or email from Fello, an admin had to set up the whole phone system behind it: buying phone numbers and Copilot Seats, porting existing numbers, and clearing the compliance needed to legally use them.

The compliance was the tricky bit. It meant business registration, CNAM (caller-ID) setup, number-integrity checks and 10DLC registration, and 10DLC alone asked the admin to spell out what the number was for, how they'd use it, write sample messages, and work through a string of smaller steps. Each was a long, intimidating flow on its own, and a busy real-estate admin had little patience for any of them.

Multiple flows → one three-step setup.

Take a stack of required, multi-step regulatory processes and make them feel like a quick, guided setup, without losing any of the compliance the platform actually needed.

I got there by combining steps that belonged together, auto-filling info the platform already had, and using AI to draft the sample messages 10DLC needs, so the admin reviewed and confirmed instead of writing everything from scratch.

How I approached it.

A few simple rules kept things on track as the flows got shorter.

01 Combine what belongs together Group the smaller steps into single, clear moments instead of walking the admin through each one.
02 Auto-fill what's already known Pre-fill every field the platform already had, so the admin just confirms instead of re-typing.
03 Let AI do the heavy lifting Use AI to draft the sample messages 10DLC needs, turning writing into a quick review.

The wider surface.

The setup flow was part of a bigger set of communication experiences I designed.

Number & seat setup Buying numbers and Copilot Seats, number porting, and the business registration, CNAM and 10DLC compliance behind them.
Team management Admin tools to add and remove Copilot Seats, manage permissions, and assign phone numbers across a team.
Call settings Voicemail, call forwarding and related controls, for individuals and at the admin level.

Grounded in research.

This wasn't a space I could design on gut feel; calling and messaging is a mature, heavily regulated area with a lot of established patterns. So I did a fair bit of competitive research, looking at dedicated calling platforms and at CRMs that build communication into the product.

Dedicated calling platforms
Aircall JustCall
How mature telephony products handle buying numbers, porting and compliance as a first-class setup experience.
CRMs with built-in comms
Follow Up Boss HubSpot
How communication setup sits inside a broader CRM, where it competes for an admin's limited attention.

Seeing how each one handled buying numbers, porting and compliance helped me borrow what worked, skip the dead ends, and land on a flow that fit Fello's users rather than copying any one of them.

Working with the team.

This was a genuinely collaborative project; I worked closely with a product manager and the product head, and a lot of what made the final flows good came out of that.

With the PM We worked out what actually mattered to the admin versus what was legacy or regulatory detail: which steps could be merged, which fields could be auto-filled, and where we could safely simplify without breaking compliance.
With the product head They gave me the bigger picture of where the platform was heading, and, since I was new, helped me understand what the AI could actually do here and how much we could realistically automate.

Knowing that first-hand is what made the step reductions real; they weren't wishful thinking, they were grounded in what the system could genuinely pull off.

Also at Fello.

Beyond the communication setup, I owned two other core pieces of the platform.

Redesigning the communication system I reworked how conversations work on Fello so they could handle multiple participants and recipients, moving the platform past one-to-one messaging into something that could hold real, many-sided client conversations.
CRM integration & setup Simplifying the two-way data sync between Fello and an agency's existing CRM, refocusing what got shared while keeping users in control, and turning a dense permission flow into a single guided step.

What I'd measure next.

I moved on before these flows shipped, so I can't speak to how they performed once live, and I'd rather be upfront about that than overclaim. If I'd stayed through launch, here's what I'd have wanted to know.

01
Setup completion. How many admins get through the full compliance setup on their own, versus dropping off partway.
02
Time to first call. How quickly a new team goes from signing up to actually making calls.
03
Support & compliance load. Whether setup tickets and failed 10DLC registrations went down.
